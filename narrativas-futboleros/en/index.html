Preface

I'm not quite sure why I'm writing all this in some future (ha!); what I do feel today is that artistic manifestations often get praised as if they were a stratagem, a holy grail that existed beyond the imperfections and cronyism proper to the so-called human experience.

Well then, for the one writing these lines, making things, whether you call it art or anything else, is my way of accepting the cyclical and timeless nature of things. I write so that, at some later point, I can remember what I was doing or what troubled me at a given moment in time; I draw because I don't want to forget an image tattooed on my mind with overwhelming force; I make jokes because so many monstrous things are happening that it's the only halfway realistic avenue through which my pain can dissipate for a while.

Now then, I have a (somewhat problematic) obsession with documenting: every single idea, line, predicate or conjugation I use in language has to have its proper source, so I can trace where the idea I'm building comes from (honestly, plagiarism is nothing more than the natural formation of ideas without proper citation), as if feeding the human fabric. And up to a point that's a commendable obsession to keep for most of the things I do, but this document is anything but that idea put into practice: it is the realization of my fears, anxieties, sorrows and quirks that portray my character in the face of a leisure activity (one of the thousands I have, like a collector of feelings), football, but materialized through fiction and speculation.

Why? Well, let's start by saying that many people have already contributed substantial value on this topic, through criticism, meticulousness and research, in a very constant and respectable way, and honestly I don't think I qualify in any of those categories. Under my Damoclean mirror (not sword), I am more a maker of things than a meticulous specialist; I loathe specialization in myself, not as a concept, but my lost cause is that specialization as a concept in the service of so-called "productivity" (not that everything I make comes out well, far from it).

This futurist conception, although it doesn't enter the canon of science fiction that envisioned utopian settings, doesn't entirely stray from speculative fiction either; in fact, I try to respond to or express genuine social and cultural concerns through the lens of the scenario and its viability. But I see it somewhat in the line of what writer Octavia Butler or musician George Clinton did in his seminal project Parliament and Funkadelic, and it is my vision of an identity body, in this case that of national football, and of the power structures set in a scene that transcends our immediacy or immediate alternation. Why? To let it out, so that someone is left with these lines and several of us worry about everything that's happening; that's what the future is for, the one we perhaps will never see.

Without further ado, you are invited to scrutinize, refute, debate or simply enjoy these stormy musings from someone who perhaps enjoys Colombian football much more than they should, now or always.
