A journey through the corruption, music and chaos of Colombia

Introduction

Colombia is a sancocho: a cruel, rotten, vile and soulless concoction, simmered over the blazing fire of the national character, made of that abominable triad of corruption, indolence and ignorance that covers every corner of our country.

Just skim any newspaper and you'll find gems like Supreme Court magistrates, supposedly spotless and impeccable dispensers of justice, tangled in a grotesque web of corruption; presidential pre-candidates under investigation; healthcare getting worse every day; and, to top it all off, people who appeal to love of country in pursuit of their Machiavellian aims. A whole concept of the state and a failed homeland.

The ignominious broth that bubbles in the fetid cauldron called Colombia splashes us all equally, regardless of creed, race or sex. In our regions people talk about corruption on equal terms, of course, though each region adds its own shade according to our beloved national character, whatever it may be: chatty and charming Paisa politicians; from the capital, the classic style of the dandy Bogotano, very sober and used to the jet set; further east, in our bled-dry Pacific, the bureaucrat disguised as a representative of minorities; and so we finally arrive at the Caribbean coast.

The Colombian Caribbean has been under the magnifying glass for a long time thanks to its web of political graft: from the Gulf of Urabá to the Guajira peninsula, there is no nook of this territory untouched by the problem. Reasons? Many: from the region's appeal to domestic and foreign investment, to the state's absence at key points, and the power of family clans that have never let go of the nursing breast of state subsidy and the bureaucratic cake.

The national media have coined the word 'mermelada' (jam) for the budget allocations that flow from the government to mayors and governors at the request of legislators from each territory, supposedly to make necessary investments in each place, but which in reality do not follow the Development Plans.

Today I propose to everyone reading this that we coin the term 'sancocho' to refer to those grotesque payments of political quotas or favors in the form of public money (in the end the term is coined for our region). Let's use that deeply rooted dish of culinary fusion, which mixes Spanish stews like 'la olla podrida' (imagine that) with the ajiacos that in ancient times represented the most excellent of the ancestral techniques of our colonized Indigenous peoples. Unlike jam, the sancocho has a communal origin: it is cooked, then shared, and finally ferociously devoured by the diners.

But so as not to keep stirring up tempers with these grim notes about the complicated national present, today I have a bestiary for you, built from head to toe on two elements which, like 'jam' (sorry, 'sancocho') are so rooted in our colorful Caribbean people: cooking and vallenato.

The choice of these themes aims to expose the true nature of the Caribbean region: gluttony (unless we're talking about La Guajira, where there is nothing to invent), drink (of parrandas and gatherings among the region's political camps) and music, three elements that coexist in an almost perfect harmony that has not been written about enough to this day. And above all, let's finally give ourselves a moment to laugh amid all the chaos that drowns us every day in this cruel and apathetic country.
